# Product

## Register

product

## Users

360Ghar serves property seekers evaluating homes from a mobile-first real estate app. Users are browsing listings, comparing location and property details, saving or passing options, scheduling visits, and managing their account preferences while likely moving between short discovery sessions and deeper property review.

## Product Purpose

The app helps users discover, evaluate, and act on real estate listings through a swipe-first discovery flow, map exploration, property details, virtual tours, liked/passed history, visit scheduling, profile management, and utility calculators. Success means a user can quickly find relevant properties, understand each listing, preserve intent, and move toward a visit or agent interaction without confusion.

## Brand Personality

Premium, practical, trustworthy. The interface should feel polished enough for high-consideration real estate decisions while keeping workflows direct, readable, and predictable.

## Anti-references

Avoid marketing-page theatrics inside task surfaces, decorative motion that slows down browsing, inconsistent button/form patterns across screens, vague empty states, and real estate UI that hides concrete listing details behind visual polish.

## Design Principles

- Preserve decision momentum: browsing, filtering, liking, passing, and scheduling should stay fast.
- Make listing data inspectable: price, location, media, features, and next actions should be easy to scan.
- Use familiar product affordances: navigation, tabs, forms, dialogs, and error states should behave predictably.
- Keep premium restraint: brand gold and editorial accents should clarify primary actions and state, not decorate every surface.
- Recover clearly: offline, loading, empty, permission-denied, auth, and API-error states should explain what happened and what the user can do next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use a practical WCAG AA baseline for mobile product UI: readable contrast, scalable text, tappable controls, clear focus/semantic labels where applicable, reduced-motion-friendly transitions, and no color-only communication for status.
